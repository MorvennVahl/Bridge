# exp01 — The degree floor, and whether it survives the label definition

**Lineage.** `lab/round_01.md` lanes L1 (degree-only baseline) and L8 (label-definition
sensitivity), merged: the model is so small that running it across five label variants
costs less than running it once across two experiments.

**Status.** Not started. No dependency on any other experiment; every later experiment
reports its AP next to this one's.

---

## Question

How much of the harm label is explained by observation frequency alone, and is that number
an artefact of the Evans thresholds?

## Hypothesis

Three counts — drug degree, condition degree, and the condition's OHDSI network
`record_count` — reach an average precision **well above the 11.0% prevalence** on
`y_faers_signal`, and the *ranking* of those three features by their individual AP is
**stable across all five label definitions**.

**What would falsify it.** Either half can fail independently, and both failures matter:

- If degree-only AP is close to prevalence, then reporting bias is not the dominant
  structure in this label and the warnings in `AGENT.md` §5 are overstated. Later
  experiments would have a much easier floor to clear than we assumed.
- If the feature ranking flips between label definitions — condition degree carries the
  Evans label but drug degree carries the `cases >= 10` label, say — then every subsequent
  result in the project has to be reported with this sensitivity attached, and no single
  headline AP is meaningful on its own.

## Why it goes first

It produces the reference number for the whole project. It also tells the lab, cheaply,
whether the label choice is a free parameter we can stop thinking about or a confound we
have to carry through every later report.

## Inputs

| path on the volume | rows | why |

`record_count` is an occurrence frequency from the OHDSI network via Hecate — an external
measure, not a function of these labels — which is why it is allowed here while the CEM
degree terms in `ingredient_features_label_adjacent.csv` are not.

**If `condition_features_basic.csv` is absent** (it is regenerated by another session):
run with the two train-derived degree terms only, report that you did, and leave the
`record_count` row of the results table empty. Do not substitute a CEM-derived count for
it — that is the quarantined feature under a different name.

## Method

1. Compute the two degree terms on **train only**: `drug_degree` = distinct conditions per
   ingredient, `condition_degree` = distinct ingredients per condition. Apply the same
   mapping to validate; conditions absent from train get the train median. Use `log1p` of
   every count.
2. Build the five label variants, all from columns already in the split files:

   | id | definition | train positive rate |
   |---|---|---|
   | `evans` | `faers_prr >= 2 & faers_chi_square >= 4 & faers_case_count >= 3` (= `y_faers_signal`) | 0.1100 |
   | `prr2` | `faers_prr >= 2` alone | 0.3629 |
   | `prr4_c5` | `faers_prr >= 4 & faers_case_count >= 5` | 0.0355 |
   | `cases10` | `faers_case_count >= 10`, any PRR | 0.2161 |
   | `any_harm` | `y_any_harm` (FAERS signal ∪ SemMedDB harm) | 0.1110 |

   Those rates are measured on the written train file; assert them at startup as a
   precondition check, to ±0.001. A mismatch means the split was rebuilt underneath you.
3. For each label, fit two models: logistic regression on the log counts, and
   `HistGradientBoostingClassifier(max_iter=200, learning_rate=0.06, max_leaf_nodes=63,
   early_stopping=False)`. The gap between them is the amount of non-linearity three counts
   can support; report it as a number, not a remark.
4. For each label, also fit each feature **alone** (three single-feature models), so the
   table shows which count carries the signal rather than only that some count does.
5. Grouped 3-fold CV inside train on `group_key` for the reported train estimate. Then one
   pass on validate.
6. `y_semmeddb_causes` is **out of scope here** — 715 train positives is too few for this
   grid to say anything, and exp05 handles the sparse-label regime properly.

## Budget

| step | est. |
|---|---|
| read splits + build features | 40 s |
| 5 labels × (2 models × (3 CV + 1 full) + 3 single-feature fits) | ~2.5 min (3-feature fits are seconds each) |
| calibration + figures | 30 s |
| **total** | **~4 min** |

`timeout=600`, `cpu=8.0`, `memory=16384`. Comfortably inside budget — this is the one
experiment in the round with slack, so if you want to add a variant, add it here and say
what it was.

## Deliverables

Written to `/results/<exp_id>/`:

- `<exp_id>_ap_table.csv` — one row per (label, model, feature set): AP, ROC-AUC,
  prevalence, AP lift over prevalence, train CV AP with its fold spread
- `<exp_id>_feature_ranking.csv` — single-feature AP per label, and whether the ranking is
  stable across labels (state it as a Kendall tau between label columns, not as an
  impression)
- `<exp_id>_calibration.png` — reliability curve on validate for the Evans label, both
  models, 20 bins
- `<exp_id>_subgroups.csv` — validate AP split by `is_mapped`, `arm`, and
  `best_match_tier`, for the Evans label

## Reporting requirements

`metrics` must include `validate_average_precision` (Evans, LightGBM/HistGBM — this is the
number the leaderboard ranks on), `train_cv_average_precision`,
`baseline_degree_only_ap` (identical to the headline here, stated for schema consistency),
plus one `ap_<label>` per variant.

`findings` must state: the Evans AP against the 11.7% validate prevalence; which single
count carries the most signal; the logistic-vs-boosting gap; and **explicitly** whether the
feature ranking held across the five label definitions. If it held, say that later
experiments may report a single label; if it flipped, say which pair flipped and that the
sensitivity is now mandatory downstream.

## Traps specific to this experiment

- **Do not compute degree over the concatenation of train and validate.** It is the
  fastest way to produce an impressive and worthless number in this project.
- `faers_ror` is identical to `faers_prr` in this extract — it is not a second measure, so
  do not build a label variant from it.
- `cases10` has a 21.6% positive rate and is close to "this pair was reported often". It is
  in the panel as the deliberately most bias-dominated end of the spectrum, not as a
  candidate label for the project.
- The 1,187 unmapped conditions carry disproportionately many pairs and their
  `record_count` behaviour differs; the `subgroups.csv` deliverable exists so this shows up
  rather than being averaged away.
